We would rather you understand the report's limits than overtrust it. A source that states its own boundaries is usually one you can rely on within them.

## What makes it reliable

- **It predates the dispute.** The record was sealed and timestamped when the track was created, not assembled after a flag was raised. The evidence cannot have been reverse-engineered to win an argument.
- **It is tamper-evident.** The signed C2PA record would fail verification if the file had been altered after sealing. Intact verification means you are seeing what was sealed.
- **It is independently checkable.** You do not have to trust SessionSeal's rendering of it. You can open the credential in any standard C2PA verifier, and you can listen to the stems yourself. See [How do I check the C2PA credential myself?](/faq/how-to-check-c2pa).
- **It is honest about uncertainty.** The verdict follows the evidence rather than always reading "strong," missing signals never count against a record, and only genuine contradictions lower the score. See [How does SessionSeal score this?](/faq/how-scoring-works).

## Its limits

- **It evidences studio work, not authorship.** It shows the artifacts of real production; it does not certify who wrote the music. See [Does this page prove a human made it?](/faq/does-this-prove-a-human-made-it).
- **The signer is currently self-attested.** This does not weaken the integrity or timing guarantees, but a public certificate authority does not yet vouch for the signing key. See [Should I trust a "self-attested" signature?](/faq/self-attested-signature).
- **A weak or inconsistent verdict means what it says.** If the report does not show strong evidence, do not treat it as if it does. The bands are meant to be read literally.

## How to use it

Use the report as **strong, timestamped, independently verifiable evidence that a real studio process produced the track**, which is precisely what a false AI flag on a genuine record needs rebutting with. Weigh it alongside anything else you would normally consider. Where the evidence is strong and you can confirm the stems yourself, it is reasonable grounds to conclude an automated AI flag is a false positive.
