This is the most important thing to understand before you rely on the page, so we want to be direct about it.

## What it does not prove

The report does **not** prove authorship. No tool can look at an audio file and certify that a specific human, and no AI, wrote the melody. Anyone who tells you otherwise is overselling. SessionSeal does not make that claim.

## What it does prove

It proves that the track is accompanied by the **artifacts of real studio production**, and that those artifacts are internally consistent and were sealed before any dispute:

- separate instrument and vocal stems that mix down to the finished master,
- a project session (the producer's DAW file) that the stems trace back to,
- signs of an actual working process: edit history, recordings made across multiple days, leftover material that never shipped.

An AI music generator outputs a single finished file. It has no stems, no session, no edit history, no multi-day recording dates. So when a record carries all of that, and it holds together, you are looking at the footprint of real studio work.

## Why that is usually what you need

Most AI flags on genuine tracks are false positives from an automated classifier. You are not trying to prove the philosophical question of authorship; you are trying to decide whether an automated "this looks AI" flag holds up. Evidence of real, multi-track, multi-day studio production is strong, concrete grounds to conclude it does not.

> Use it as: strong evidence that real studio work produced this track. Not as: a certificate of authorship.
