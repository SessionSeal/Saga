To read the evidence on a SessionSeal report, it helps to know what real production leaves behind that an AI generator does not.

## What an AI generator gives you

You type a prompt, and a model returns **one finished audio file**. That is the entire output. There is no project behind it, because no project was ever opened. There are no separate tracks, because the model rendered the mix directly. There is no history, because it was produced in one shot.

## What real studio production leaves behind

A track made in a studio drags a long tail of byproducts with it:

- **Stems.** The song exists first as separate layers, such as drums, bass, vocals, and guitars, that are mixed together into the master. If those layers exist and genuinely mix down to the finished track, something built the track out of parts.
- **A session file.** The producer's DAW project (Logic, Ableton, and so on) that references the recordings, arranges them, and holds the mix. It is editable and inspectable.
- **Edit history.** DAWs save backups and versions as you work. Their presence shows the track was built up over time, not generated instantly.
- **Multiple recording days.** Recorded audio files carry the date they were captured. Real production usually spans more than one day. Instant generation cannot.
- **Leftovers.** Real sessions are messy: alternate takes, scrapped ideas, unused files. An AI export contains only itself.

## Why this framing matters

None of these, alone, is absolute proof. But they are things that **cost real time and effort to produce, and that an AI export simply does not have**. When a record presents several of them and they are mutually consistent, faking all of it convincingly is far harder than just making the music for real. That is the logic the whole report is built on.
