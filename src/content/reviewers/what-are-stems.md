If the report lets you listen to stems, that section is often the fastest way to satisfy yourself, because you are checking with your own ears rather than trusting a number.

## What a stem is

A finished song is a mix of many separate recordings played together: a drum track, a bass track, one or more vocal tracks, guitars, keys, and so on. Each of those individual layers is a **stem**. The producer records and edits them separately, then mixes them down into the single stereo file you hear on streaming services.

## Why hearing them isolated is strong evidence

An AI music generator outputs the **finished mix only**. It never produces the underlying layers, because it never worked in layers; it rendered the final audio directly from a model.

So if you can play a track's drums with no vocals, then its vocals with no drums, and each sounds like a clean, separate recording, you are hearing something an AI export cannot give you. Multi-track production is exactly what real studio work is.

## What to listen for

- Each stem should sound like **one part of the song in isolation**: just the drums, just the vocal, and so on.
- Played together, the stems should reconstruct the released track.
- Real recorded stems often carry natural detail: room sound, breaths before a vocal line, small performance imperfections. These are hallmarks of a recording, not a rendered mix.
